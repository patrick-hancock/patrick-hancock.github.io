Kestrel Valley Residential: technical write-up

A claim-checked BI system for a property management company, with the misses shown

How the Kestrel Valley case study is built and tested: the data flow, the four views, how each figure was checked, how to rerun the checks, and every limit recorded during the build.

Synthetic data, fictional company

Designed, specified and validated by me; built with AI-assisted development.

Kestrel Valley is a fictional property management company built on synthetic data. All 895 numbers on its claim cards were rechecked against the source records, and none of the 3 look-alike decoys was blamed for a problem. The system found 4 of its 7 planted causes and passed 38 of 58 assistant questions, up from 32 on the first run, with every miss listed. I designed the Databricks marts, the claim cards, four views and a grounded assistant.

The views and the assistant

Kestrel Valley Residential is a fictional property management company. This system is the reporting and analysis built for it, on realistic made-up records. Seven real causes were hidden in the data on purpose, along with three look-alikes that are not causes, so every finding the system makes can be checked against a known answer.

  • Property manager

    Fifteen pages: all 14 communities in order of need, then one page for each community on its units, leases, repairs and spending against its own plan. Reached from the regional view.

  • Regional manager

    Five pages on market position, repairs and service, staffing, leasing and what residents are saying.

  • Asset manager

    Six pages on occupancy, costs, the plan, capital and debt for one asset.

  • Owner

    Four pages on the assets, capital and debt, in the owner's terms.

  • Grounded assistant

    Answers from the handbook, a measure or a claim card, and refuses when none of them supports an answer.

What the system does

For each community, it compares results with the local market and with the community's own past, then tests possible explanations against the evidence. It writes a finding card for each, called a claim card. A card either names a cause, rules a suspect out, or says the evidence is not enough.

Why it can be trusted

Every card carries the query behind each number, and a card that cannot name a cause says so instead of guessing. The sections below say what was found, what was missed and what the testing did and did not show.

  • Assistant questions passed

    38 of 58

    After one fix to how business measures are read

    How each figure was tested: Assistant questions passed

    The assistant answered 60 prepared questions, and 58 count toward the score. A question passes when the answer comes from the right source, or the assistant correctly declines to answer. The first run passed 32 of 58, and six more passed once business measures were read from the shared definitions. 38 of 58 is now the lowest score a later version may have. It is not a target.

    • Questions that should be answered: 25 of 41 passed. Questions that should be declined: 13 of 17 passed.
    • By where the answer comes from: handbook 13 of 17, business measures 10 of 11, claim cards 2 of 13.
    • Two questions wait on handbook policy numbers and are left out of the 58.

    Assistant test, 20 Nov 2026

  • Same answer every time

    20 of 20

    Repeatable, which is not the same as reliable

    How each figure was tested: Same answer every time

    The 20 hardest questions were each asked 3 times and gave exactly the same answer every time. Most of these 20 are questions the assistant fails, so most of the repeats are the same wrong answer given again.

    • The assistant follows fixed rules and does not use a language model, so identical answers were expected.
    • This does not show how it would do with a language model writing the answers, or with new questions.

How each figure was tested

4 of 7 hidden causes found

What was tested
The 59 claim cards were checked against the seven causes planted in the synthetic data.
How the expected answers were set
An answer key of seven causes and three look-alikes, which I picked on 18 November 2026, was written before the blind score.
What counted as correct
A cause counts as found when a card at that property overlaps the planted period by at least one month, names the mapped cause type in the expected direction, and has a confidence of at least 0.55.
What failed
The first blind score found 3 of 7. After the fix pass three causes were still missed, and the checker report gives the reason for each.
What changed after
One fix pass followed, with each rule change named after the miss that exposed it. The answer key was known during that pass, and the rules were not tuned further.
How to rerun it
python checks/check_claims.py --answer-key scenarios/answer_key.json

3 of 3 look-alikes not blamed

What was tested
The three planted look-alikes, which resemble causes but are not, were checked against every claim card.
How the expected answers were set
The same answer key, written before the blind score.
What counted as correct
A look-alike counts as wrongly blamed when a card names it as a cause, and the checker requires that none is.
What failed
None was blamed. The checker also reports that no card ruled any of the three out by name, which it records but does not gate.
What changed after
Nothing is recorded as changed for the look-alikes.
How to rerun it
python checks/check_claims.py --answer-key scenarios/answer_key.json

895 of 895 card numbers rechecked

What was tested
Every gated number on every card was recalculated from the source records without the scorer's own metric code, covering 444 observed values, 206 baselines, 158 gaps and 87 from-and-to pairs.
How the expected answers were set
The value recalculated from the source records.
What counted as correct
A number matches when it is within 0.01 or 0.5 percent of the recalculated value, whichever is larger.
What failed
The first run could recalculate only 35.5 percent of the numbers and found 3 mismatches. The final run has none. A control run with values inflated by about 3 percent produced 478 mismatches, which shows the check can fail.
What changed after
The checker was extended to rebuild every kind of metric on the cards.
How to rerun it
python checks/check_claims.py --answer-key scenarios/answer_key.json

38 of 58 assistant questions passed

What was tested
Sixty prepared questions ran through the assistant, and 58 count, because two wait on handbook policy numbers.
How the expected answers were set
Each question carries an expected status, source and wording checks. Card questions are bound to card ids, and measure questions to the shared measure values with no tolerance.
What counted as correct
The answer comes from the right source with the expected citation and wording, or the assistant correctly declines.
What failed
Twenty questions failed. Five of them depend on the three causes the checker missed, and across all the failures nine are refusals where an answer was expected and one is a wrong answer.
What changed after
The first run passed 32 of 58. Reading business measures from the shared definitions turned six measure questions to pass and none to fail, which gave 38 of 58. That score is a floor for later versions, not a target.
How to rerun it
python assistant/evals/run_evals.py, then python assistant/evals/score.py --report assistant/evals/results.md

20 of 20 gave the same answer every time

What was tested
The 20 hardest questions, chosen by script from the first run with failures first and then the smallest retrieval margin, were each asked three times.
How the expected answers were set
Identical answers were expected, because the assistant follows fixed rules and calls no language model.
What counted as correct
The three records for each question are byte-identical.
What failed
Before a fix, one question's evidence came back in a different order between runs. Most of the 20 are questions the assistant fails, so most repeats are the same wrong answer.
What changed after
The row order was made stable, and all 20 then repeated exactly.
How to rerun it
python assistant/evals/run_evals.py, then python checks/check_runs.py

Honest limits

The pages say plainly what the evidence does not yet support.

AreaWhat it does not show
DataMade up, with no real company's figures
Hidden causes4 of 7 found after one round of fixes with the answers known, and none of the 3 look-alikes wrongly blamed
Known misses5 assistant questions depend on the three causes the checker missed, so they fail by design
Repeat runs20 of 20 gave the same answer each time, which shows it is repeatable, not that it is reliable
On hold2 questions wait on handbook policy numbers and are not counted in the 58
Loan maturity outlook (owner view)Shown as debt coverage, debt yield and months until the loan is due, not as a forecast
Market growth (asset view)Shows the market's growth only
Return on capital (asset view)There is no claim-card type for capital return, so none is shown
Repair wait times (regional view)The typical wait is the lower of the two middle values when the count is even
Loans past their due dateShow refinanced instead of a negative number of months

These limits were decided and recorded as the system was built, and each is shown on the page it affects.
