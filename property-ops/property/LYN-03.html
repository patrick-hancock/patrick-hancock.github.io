How this is measured. The answer sentence and the priorities follow one published order of five headline measures: 60-day exposure above the community's own average for the 12 months before; vacant units not ready; work orders past target; leases ending within 60 days with no renewal signed; controllable expenses over plan for the year so far. Communities are sorted by how many of the five are over target, then by 60-day exposure. Figures are monthly and read as of October 2026; there is no weekly or daily view. Leased percentage is physical occupancy, because the rent roll records only occupied and vacant units. Exposure is a forecast, not a count: it takes the vacant units plus the leases ending within the window with no renewal signed, scales those leases by one minus the community's renewal rate over the last 12 months, and shows the result as a share of units. It is a model; the raw count of leases ending is printed beside it. Where no lease ended in the last 12 months there is no renewal rate, so the forecast shows a dash. There is no notice field in the data, so the raw count overstates the units that will really come open. Past due is the resident ledger balance as a share of the month's charges, and units are grouped as 1-30, 31-60 and 61 or more days past due; ledger balances are not comparable with lease rent. A building walk is overdue when more than 14 days have passed since the last one (a bi-weekly walk). Vacant, not ready means the unit has a make-ready turn still open. Work orders are past target after 1, 3 and 7 days for emergency, urgent and routine priority. Units are shown by unit id; no resident names appear.
